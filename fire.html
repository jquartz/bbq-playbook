OVERNIGHT FIRE MANAGEMENT

A rectangular fuse, not blind faith.

The snake/fuse helps stretch burn time, but it does not turn the grill into a set-and-forget smoker. Plan checks and use a remote alarm if available.

BUILD

Friday setup

  1. Use briquettes.

    More uniform and predictable than lumpwood for the overnight brisket.

  2. Build a long C/U-shaped path.

    Unlit briquettes along the charcoal area edge, not one giant lit pile.

  3. Light 10–12 briquettes.

    Place them at one end to start a controlled progression.

  4. Add 5–6 oak chunks.

    Place through the first two-thirds. Do not soak them.

  5. Keep the exhaust open.

    Control heat mainly with the intake. Small changes, then wait.

Pit target
115–120°C

Working band: roughly 110–130°C.

Overnight safety: keep the cooker outdoors in a well-ventilated, fire-safe position away from combustible structures. Do not treat any charcoal setup as fully unattended.

RECOVERY GUIDE

When the pit misbehaves.

Below 105°C

Fuel or oxygen

Open intake a little. Confirm the fuse is burning. Add a small amount of lit fuel if progression has stalled. Wait before changing again.

Above 135°C

Reduce intake

Close intake somewhat, but do not choke the exhaust. Avoid opening the lid as a temperature-control habit.

Bark ready early

Wrap and move indoors

Once bark is set, the oven is the safer, more predictable way to finish. Smoke flavour is already established.

Bark not ready

Ignore the clock

Do not wrap simply because a timetable says so. If the bark is still soft/pale, give it more pit time.

Brisket finishes early

Good problem

Vent briefly, then hold wrapped and warm. Long warm holding is preferable to rushing a late brisket.

Rain / wind

Protect, don't enclose

Shield the cooker from direct wind/rain while preserving full outdoor ventilation and fire clearance.

RECIPES

Rub by meat, not habit.

A cohesive smokehouse flavour profile: pepper-forward beef, sweeter pork, savoury oxtail, bright chimichurri and acidic sides.

BEEF · 7.5 KG WHOLE PACKER

Texas Brisket

Rub

  • 90 g coarse black pepper
  • 90 g coarse / kosher salt
  • 15 g garlic powder, optional

Method

  1. Trim hard fat and ragged paper-thin edges; leave about 6 mm fat cap.
  2. Season evenly.
  3. Smoke at 115–120°C with oak.
  4. When bark is dark and set, typically 70–75°C internal, wrap in pink butcher paper with 2–3 tbsp warm beef tallow.
  5. Finish at 135–140°C until the thick flat probes with very little resistance, typically around 93–96°C.
  6. Vent 20–30 minutes, then hold warm. Do not slice early.

Thin-flat insurance: place the point toward the hotter side and the thin flat away from it. If the very tip is extremely thin, trim it or fold/tuck it under itself.

BRISKET POINT

Sticky Burnt Ends

Tray mix

  • Cooked brisket point, cubed 3–4 cm
  • 200–250 ml BBQ sauce
  • 3 tbsp melted beef tallow
  • 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
  • 1–2 tbsp brisket rub
  • 1 tbsp soft brown sugar, optional

Method

  1. Toss gently.
  2. Spread in a shallow tray rather than piling.
  3. Cook uncovered around 150°C for 60–90 minutes.
  4. Stir once or twice and stop when glossy, tacky and caramelised.
PORK · 4 KG BONELESS SHOULDER

Pulled Pork

Rub

  • 4 tbsp smoked or sweet paprika
  • 3 tbsp dark brown sugar
  • 2 tbsp coarse salt
  • 2 tbsp black pepper
  • 1 tbsp garlic powder
  • 1 tbsp onion powder
  • 2 tsp mustard powder
  • 2 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp cayenne, optional

Method

  1. Season generously.
  2. Smoke at 120–130°C for roughly 4–5 hours or until bark is set.
  3. Wrap with ~100 ml apple juice and 2–3 tbsp BBQ sauce.
  4. Finish around 140°C until 93–96°C and very easy to pull.
  5. Rest at least an hour; mix bark through the pulled meat.
BEEF · 6 INDIVIDUAL SHORT RIBS

Smoky Short Ribs

Adapted for a longer smoker stage from Tom Kerridge's published smoky beef short ribs. The sugar is deliberately reduced because your ribs spend hours exposed to smoker heat rather than being mostly covered in an oven. Original recipe ↗

Rub — 6 ribs

  • 2 tsp soft light brown sugar
  • 1½ tsp garlic powder
  • 1½ tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tbsp sweet smoked paprika
  • 1½ tsp onion powder
  • ¾ tsp cracked black pepper
  • 1½ tsp salt

Wrap / finish liquid

  • 150 ml malt vinegar
  • 225 ml apple juice
  • 3 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
  • Smoky BBQ sauce for final glazing

Method

  1. Season ribs.
  2. Smoke 120–130°C until deeply coloured and bark is set, about 3–4 hours.
  3. Wrap with a modest splash of the vinegar/apple/Worcestershire mixture; do not drown them.
  4. Finish until a probe slides between the fibres with little resistance.
  5. Optional: glaze with BBQ sauce and set over indirect heat for 10–15 minutes.
BEEF · 2 KG OXTAIL

Smoked Tallow-Confit Oxtail

Rub

  • 2 tbsp coarse black pepper
  • 2 tbsp coarse salt
  • 1 tbsp garlic powder
  • 1 tbsp onion powder
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1 tsp dried thyme
  • ½ tsp finely crushed rosemary

Confit

  • 2.5–3.5 kg beef tallow, enough to submerge
  • 1 garlic bulb, halved
  • 6 thyme sprigs
  • 3 rosemary sprigs
  • 3 bay leaves
  • 1 tbsp black peppercorns

Method

  1. Smoke 120–130°C for 2–3 hours until bark forms.
  2. Pack snugly in a deep vessel.
  3. Cover completely with warm tallow and aromatics.
  4. Cover and confit 120–130°C for about 5–6 hours.
  5. Stop when very tender but still liftable.
  6. Cool safely in tallow and reheat gently Sunday.

LAST-MINUTE · 8–10 SPLIT BONES

Roasted Bone Marrow

Ingredients

  • 8–10 split beef marrow bones
  • Flaky salt
  • Black pepper, optional
  • Chimichurri
  • Bread/toast, optional

Method

  1. Keep bones chilled until close to service.
  2. Pat dry, season lightly and roast cut-side up at 220–230°C for roughly 15–20 minutes.
  3. Pull when bubbling and softened but before the marrow renders away.
  4. Finish with flaky salt and serve immediately with chimichurri.

Best appliance: gas BBQ used as an indirect high-heat roasting zone. Put bones in a sturdy tray to catch rendered fat and reduce flare-ups.

SIDES

Acid, crunch, comfort.

SUNDAY MORNING · PROPOSED QUANTITY FOR 20

Creamy Dill Coleslaw

Adapted from BBC Good Food's classic homemade coleslaw, using your chosen ingredients: spring onions, carrots, cabbage, mayonnaise, salad cream, lemon juice, white wine vinegar, Dijon mustard and dill. Source inspiration ↗

Suggested batch

  • 1 large white cabbage
  • 6 carrots
  • 6 spring onions
  • 150 g mayonnaise
  • 100 g salad cream
  • 2 tbsp Dijon mustard
  • 2 tbsp white wine vinegar
  • 2 tbsp lemon juice
  • 1 large bunch dill
  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Finely shred cabbage and julienne/grate carrots.
  2. Slice spring onions and dill.
  3. Whisk the wet ingredients, season, then fold through the vegetables.
  4. Chill and stir again before service. Adjust acidity and salt at the end.

PREP SATURDAY · BAKE SUNDAY

Mac & Cheese with Brown-Butter Herb Panko

Panko topping

  • 100 g butter
  • 150 g panko breadcrumbs total
  • 5 sage leaves
  • 2 g fresh thyme
  • Salt & pepper

Macaroni

  • 2 L water
  • 20 g salt
  • 500 g macaroni

Cheese sauce

  • 600 g white wine
  • 600 g chicken stock
  • 1 thyme sprig
  • 1 rosemary sprig
  • 1 tbsp black peppercorns
  • 100 g Gruyère
  • 100 g American cheese slices
  • 30 g cornflour
  • 160 g soft cream cheese
  • 5 g Dijon mustard

Method

  1. Brown the butter until nutty; add chopped sage and picked thyme, cool slightly and mix with panko.
  2. Cook macaroni al dente in salted water; drain.
  3. Bring chicken stock with thyme, rosemary and peppercorns to the boil, then switch off and infuse.
  4. Reduce wine to about 60 g.
  5. Strain stock into wine reduction; keep on low heat.
  6. Slice American cheese and grate half the Gruyère. Toss both with cornflour.
  7. Whisk cheeses into the stock/wine base until melted and thickened.
  8. Whisk a little sauce with cream cheese and Dijon, then return to the pan.
  9. Fold in macaroni and cubed remaining Gruyère; season.
  10. Transfer to oven dish and chill. Sunday: top with panko and bake 180°C about 15 minutes until golden.

With a very meat-heavy buffet, the 500 g pasta batch gives roughly 16–20 modest scoops. Make 1½× if you want mac & cheese to be a major side.

PREP SATURDAY · BAKE SUNDAY

Smoky Butter Beans with Bacon

Ingredients

  • 4 cans butter beans
  • 225 g bacon
  • 3 spring onions
  • ¼ cup finely diced green pepper
  • 2 tbsp diced seeded jalapeño
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 240 ml chicken stock
  • 1 tbsp ground cumin
  • 1 tbsp paprika
  • 2 tsp dried oregano
  • 120 ml ketchup
  • 50–60 g brown sugar
  • 1 tbsp molasses
  • 1 tbsp yellow mustard
  • 2 tbsp apple cider vinegar
  • 1 tbsp hot sauce
  • 1 dried New Mexico or guajillo chilli
  • 2 bay leaves
  • Salt & pepper

Method

  1. Drain/rinse beans.
  2. Fry bacon crisp; reserve.
  3. Sauté spring onions, green pepper, jalapeño and garlic in bacon fat.
  4. Bloom cumin, paprika and oregano.
  5. Soften dried chilli in hot stock, then blend into the stock.
  6. Add stock, ketchup, sugar, molasses, mustard, vinegar and hot sauce; return bacon and bay leaves.
  7. Simmer 5–10 minutes, fold in beans and season.
  8. For this event, cool and refrigerate after the stovetop stage. Sunday: bake/reheat uncovered at 180°C until hot and bubbling.
  9. Rest 15 minutes before serving.
SUNDAY

Rosemary Cubed Potatoes

Suggested batch

  • 3 kg floury potatoes
  • 5–6 tbsp oil
  • Salt
  • Coarse black pepper
  • 4–6 rosemary sprigs

Method

  1. Cut 2–3 cm cubes.
  2. Parboil 5–7 minutes for extra crispness; steam-dry.
  3. Toss with oil, salt, pepper and rosemary.
  4. Roast 200–220°C until crisp and deeply golden.
  5. Refresh at high heat for 8–10 minutes before service.
